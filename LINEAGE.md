# LINEAGE: the series and The Multiverse School curriculum

> **Status:** draft · 2026-10-02 · Spanish version: [`LINEAGE.es.md`](LINEAGE.es.md)

This document is a map. For each chapter of the series *Discernimiento humano y creatividad soberana mediada por IA* (human discernment and sovereign creativity, mediated by AI), it says which of Liz Howard's material at The Multiverse School the chapter touches, where that material can be consulted publicly, and how the chapter relates to it. The goal is that the source material can be recognized, and that it is clear where it stops.

It is based on the school's public class pages and syllabi, checked on 2026-10-02. A more detailed internal version exists and is shared privately with Liz Howard / The Multiverse School.

## Chapter 1: *Pensamiento y Creatividad Soberanos con IA*

**Relationship:** situated adaptation of *Critical Thinking and Creativity with AI*. It draws from selected blocks in the second half of Liz's day (Workshops 04–09) and leaves out assistant configuration and automated production.

**Source**
- Class: [themultiverse.school/classes/277](https://themultiverse.school/classes/277)
- Syllabus: [HedgeDoc](https://docs.themultiverse.school/io3NrJbnRJikBJjJENfG0w)
- Recordings consulted, all listed in that syllabus:
  - 2025-12-08, morning: [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/3de6109b489c4d7db9fb86de2dba5253)
  - 2025-12-08, afternoon: [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/f8213850806e4e748efdc1dae927a374)
  - 2026-03-02, morning: [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/750c5fede7394fe5a6128b4dfe5d49cc)

  These are recordings of the same class. Liz notes the class *"changes every time"*, so each one is a version of the same course.

**Block by block**

| Series block | Liz's workshop | What is taken |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · What are we delegating? | no direct equivalent | closest is W05's question of which parts of a piece of work AI can fill and which it must not choose |
| 2 · Learning to look before asking | W04 *Critical Analysis and the Blended Voice* | locating the devices a text uses; explaining how a style works on a reader |
| 3 · Sources, influences and blended voices | W04 + W07 *Your Premise From the Warm-Up* | looking for the sources of a blended voice; tracing a claim to its primary source |
| 4 · Values turned into criteria | W05 *Values Debrief* + W07 | the gap between ideal values and the ones lived in practice; refining with named criteria |
| 5 · Diverge first, collaborate after | W06 *Creativity Warm-Up* (no AI) | combinations that clash on purpose, made by hand and without a model |
| 6 · AI as analyst, editor and collaborator | W04 + W07 | using the model as a first-pass editor, against a standard the person wrote |
| 7 · What of this can I defend as mine? | W09 *Write With a Template and Vibe Authorship* | identifying which parts of a generated artifact can be defended as one's own |

**Not carried over:** W01–W03 (assistant configuration, connectors and memory, prompt patterns), W08 *Master Plots*, W10 *Multi-Agent Story System* and the Deep Dives.

**Where Liz's material stops:** block 1 (the delegation map) has no equivalent session in her class.

## Chapter 2: *Inventiva bajo restricción con IA*

**Relationship:** there is no source course. The chapter's idea is Sandra Acevedo's and came first. Liz's material was looked up afterwards, to see what was related. The chapter's learning outcomes were not written from any piece of Liz's.

**Related material from Liz**

| Piece | Where | What it offers the chapter |
|---|---|---|
| *Scavenging Components* | [HedgeDoc](https://docs.themultiverse.school/BvvEwvSUTyeMpfJw6ssqYA), linked from *Build a Cyberdeck* | concrete cases of salvaging screens, keyboards and boards, with an honest estimate of difficulty |
| *Build a Cyberdeck* | [class 284](https://themultiverse.school/classes/284) · [HedgeDoc](https://docs.themultiverse.school/pJ6egq76SpKjzQmfSezFow) | assembling a computer from chosen parts; *The "Drawer Phone" (~$0)*: an old phone as a complete computer |
| *Solarpunk Automation* | [class 276](https://themultiverse.school/classes/276) · [HedgeDoc](https://docs.themultiverse.school/1-hNvdVTSMmUC1DB_wQ5jg) · guides [*Battery Salvage*](https://docs.themultiverse.school/iqDLWOfERn2F8Ud8OjCAhw) and [*Bill of Materials*](https://docs.themultiverse.school/Vp6mzG5BRryeQ-q7HLjbCg) | *"Repurpose old devices instead of buying new"*; an old phone as a community server; safe battery salvage; cheaper substitutions |
| *Consumer Device Rescue and Defense* | [class 240](https://themultiverse.school/classes/240) · [HedgeDoc](https://docs.themultiverse.school/av0KaZPvSIW1vHmVmP4U7A) · repo [`multiverse-device-rescue`](https://github.com/lizTheDeveloper/multiverse-device-rescue) | diagnosing and looking after your own device. It is diagnosis and defense, not hardware repair |

**Where Liz's material stops.** These pieces work with substituting parts, salvaging components and giving old devices a new use, mostly as servers. They do not treat these chapter outcomes as subjects of study:
- analyzing an object as a system of functions, materials, components and interfaces;
- functional decomposition and functional fixedness;
- bypass, adaptation and recombination;
- exaptation, meaning a component used for a new function beyond the "server" case;
- inventiveness under scarcity as something to learn.

"Technological disobedience" does not appear in this material.

## Chapter 3: *Software libre con IA*

**Relationship:** situated adaptation of *Intro to Open Source Software*, with an added systems-design layer around tool selection, dependency, cost, local/cloud tradeoffs, and composing a fit-for-purpose software stack.

**Source**
- Class: [themultiverse.school/classes/275](https://themultiverse.school/classes/275)
- Syllabus: [HedgeDoc](https://docs.themultiverse.school/5BcyfckcRDq3WUWOZvl4ng)
- Slides: [docs.themultiverse.school/p/intro-to-open-source-slides](https://docs.themultiverse.school/p/intro-to-open-source-slides), including *The Replacement Tables – Cheat Sheet*
- Recordings listed in the syllabus, among them: Feb '26, [*Exploring Open Source Software Alternatives and Budgeting for Tech Expenses*](https://www.loom.com/share/37e3586a6fb943cf86291db46504206c), and March '26, [Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/cafc5114eb10488c8b451ed8dbd37582)

**What Liz's course teaches.** A 90-minute session whose motivation is *"Stop paying for software you can get for free"*. It includes:
- an audit of the software you pay for;
- what open source means and how to tell a good project;
- finding replacements on openalternative.co;
- installing one in class;
- a monthly progression, where months 2 to 4 cover self-hosting and business-tool migration.

The facilitator notes say: *"The audience doesn't need to know about licenses, Git, or contribution workflows."*

**What is adapted: savings.** The chapter keeps savings, but frames them as **frugality: a permanent practice of managing resources**, not only as cutting spending. In Liz's course the starting point is someone who already pays for subscriptions.

**The added layer: systems design.** The chapter does not stop at swapping isolated tools. Each person works out:
- which functions they need;
- how tools work together, in series or in parallel;
- what is better kept local and what in the cloud;
- what is worth paying for;
- what dependencies each part of the system introduces.

Liz's curriculum has nearby material for AI models: choosing a model by task and deciding where it runs by cost per outcome, in [*Control AI Spending*](https://themultiverse.school/classes/249) and [*Hosting Open-Source Models*](https://themultiverse.school/classes/252). There is no equivalent for composing a chain of tools for general work.
